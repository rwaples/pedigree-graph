# Slice 5c inbreeding baseline

What `PedigreeGraph.inbreeding()` costs at production scale: the genome-node
Meuwissen-Luo walk of ADR 0008, measured across the four parity pedigrees.

## Method

Every figure below was produced by `benchmarks/bench_inbreeding.py`, which is
committed precisely so these numbers can be reproduced and challenged:

```bash
cd external/pedigree-graph
pixi run python benchmarks/bench_inbreeding.py --repeat 5 --out benchmarks/reports/inbreeding.json
pixi run python benchmarks/bench_inbreeding.py --render benchmarks/reports/inbreeding.json
```

Run these from inside the checkout. `pixi run --manifest-path <path>` selects the
environment but leaves the working directory at the umbrella root, so the
relative paths above do not resolve under it.

Each cell runs in a fresh subprocess and is repeated five times, and the table
quotes the median with the observed spread across runs. Peak RSS is the
kernel's `VmHWM`, reset through `/proc/self/clear_refs` at the start of the
timed region. The harness times the whole arm (`_harness.py:640`), so the region
is the `graph.inbreeding()` call plus the checksum and the two reported facts
over the returned array. Fixture construction happens before the region and is
excluded.

`benchmarks/.gitignore` excludes `reports/`, so the raw JSON is local-only and
this note carries the environment and spread inline.

## There is no gate, on purpose

This is a recorded baseline rather than an A/B. The MZ-naive kernel that a
gate would have compared against was deleted with ADR 0008, so there is one arm
and nothing to regress against inside this suite. ADR 0008's own wall and
memory figures came from a 1M-row simACE pedigree that is not a parity fixture
and cannot be re-derived from this repository, which is why that comparison is
not reproduced here.

The baseline exists so the ADR 0007 Rust port has something to port against.
When a native walk lands, it becomes the second arm and this suite becomes the
A/B the family 5% rule applies to.

`deep_inbred_60g` is in the sweep as the stress cell, not as filler. A
60-generation closed herd is where the walk's `touched` ancestor set grows most,
and the measurements below confirm it: 728 rows cost 7.8x what 1,020 shallow
rows cost.

## Results

| input | strategy | reps | wall (median) | spread | peak RSS (median) | checksum |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `random_1k` | genome-node Meuwissen-Luo walk | 5 | 0.00 s | 67.3% | 164 MiB | `10530816` |
| `deep_inbred_60g` | genome-node Meuwissen-Luo walk | 5 | 0.02 s | 2.0% | 164 MiB | `572836834` |
| `random_30k` | genome-node Meuwissen-Luo walk | 5 | 0.24 s | 1.0% | 171 MiB | `1068658432` |
| `random_300k` | genome-node Meuwissen-Luo walk | 5 | 2.64 s | 12.2% | 212 MiB | `11338752` |

The rendered table rounds wall time to two decimals, which is coarser than the
two cheap cells. Their medians from the same runs are 0.002811 s for
`random_1k` and 0.021956 s for `deep_inbred_60g`. `random_1k`'s 67.3% spread is
one 0.004665 s run against a 0.002773 s minimum, which is the host's noise
floor at three milliseconds rather than a property of the walk.
`random_300k`'s 12.2% is one 2.910 s run against a 2.588 s minimum.

The reported peak RSS is the whole process, and the interpreter plus numba
floor dominates it at every size. Subtracting each cell's own baseline gives
what the walk itself holds: 0.0 MiB on both small fixtures, 0.5 MiB on
`random_30k`, and 8.1 MiB on `random_300k`. The 300k figure is consistent with
the kernel's own arrays, since `F` and `D` alone are two float64 vectors of
303,000 entries, or 4.6 MiB.

## What the numbers show

The checksum is identical across all five repetitions of every cell. Read that
narrowly. `checksum_values` XORs the raw bits (`_harness.py:190`), and XOR is
blind to order and cancels values appearing an even number of times, so a stable
checksum shows each repetition produced the same *multiset* of coefficients, not
the same vector. It would not catch a regression that scattered `F` onto the
wrong graph rows, which is exactly the failure a `per_row_to_graph` port could
introduce. The row-order guarantee is held by the test suite instead, where
`tests/test_row_order.py` compares `inbreeding()` per id across permuted inputs.

The coefficients are real inbreeding, not zeros. Mean `F` is 0.0095 on
`random_1k`, 0.65 on `deep_inbred_60g`, 0.00043 on `random_30k`, and 3.68e-05
on `random_300k`; max `F` is 0.172, 0.943, 0.127, and 0.127 respectively. The
closed herd's mean of 0.65 and max of 0.94 are what 60 generations of eight
founders produce, and they are the reason that fixture is the stress cell.

Cost tracks rows almost linearly on the wide pedigrees. Ten times the rows,
from 30,300 to 303,000, costs 11.2 times the wall time, 0.236 s against 2.637 s.
Per row that is 7.8 microseconds and 8.7 microseconds.

Depth, not size, is what makes the walk expensive. `deep_inbred_60g` has 0.71
times the rows of `random_1k` and takes 7.8 times as long, 30.2 microseconds per
row against 2.8. The walk touches each individual's ancestor set, and a
60-generation closed herd makes that set nearly the whole pedigree, so the cost
per row rises with sustained depth rather than with row count. A native port
that only reduces per-row constant factors will show its largest gain on the
wide fixtures and its smallest on this one.

Nothing here is near a resource limit. The largest fixture finishes in under
three seconds and holds 8 MiB above the interpreter floor, so inbreeding is not
a bottleneck at 300k rows and has no threshold, chunking, or capacity argument
to tune.
